# Methodological critical appraisal of six source-checked conference studies

**Audit boundary:** As-of 21 November 2026, this is a **single-reviewer critical methodological note** for the six main-conference publisher PDFs previously inspected in the research process. It is **not** an independent dual-reviewer risk-of-bias judgment, a systematic appraisal of all 55 full-text candidates, or a validated risk-of-bias instrument applied to them. “Not demonstrated” means the study was not designed to identify that causal contrast, not that it is a poor paper.

The survey's distinctive criterion is *whether an answer is changed or maintained because of the strength of evidence rather than merely due to conversational or peer pressure*. Experiments can test different dimensions of that question without being interchangeable.

## Construct-validity comparison

| Publication | Controlled evidence/challenge manipulation | Truth / factual outcome | Distinguishes pressure from justified revision? | Most important interpretive limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Estornell & Liu, NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/32e07a110c6c6acf1afbf2bf82b614ad-Abstract-Conference.html) | Majority-convergence scenarios plus pruning/refutation interventions | Task-specific correctness; TruthfulQA uses an LLM judge | **Partial:** tests shared misconceptions and debate dynamics, not independently assigned peer authority | Correlated initial beliefs can mimic conformity; mix of automatic and LLM-judged outcomes |
| [Choi, Zhu & Li, NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/934252acd87f254d5d4672fbde283bd2-Abstract-Conference.html) | Independent majority voting vs actual multi-round debate and intervention conditions | Accuracy and theoretical belief-update models on seven NLP benchmark tasks | **Partial but central:** distinguishes aggregation from interactive revision, not user-directed social compliance | Interaction protocols and independence assumptions affect estimated benefit |
| [Kenton et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/899511e37a8e01e1bd6f6f1d377cc250-Abstract-Conference.html) | **Strong:** correct vs incorrect assigned advocates, debate vs one-sided consultancy vs direct judgment | A weaker LLM judge selecting the correct answer | **Yes for persuasive-agent influence** under assigned correctness, **no** for apology/acknowledgement | LLM judges are not human judges; study unit is oversight correctness |
| [Hu et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/42475c537936b2394b5015e871765056-Abstract-Conference.html) | Debate-based judging with adaptive stopping vs majority-vote baselines | Judge accuracy, stability and stopping behavior | **Not isolated:** an accuracy gain does not establish epistemically justified social revision | Independence assumption may fail under answer copying and shared biases |
| [Deng et al., CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Deng_Words_or_Vision_Do_Vision-Language_Models_Have_Blind_Faith_in_CVPR_2025_paper.html) | **Strong cross-modal:** image-only, text-only, and joint inputs; matching/corrupt/irrelevant text controls | Image-grounded task accuracy and modality preference across four tasks / ten VLMs | **Yes for image-vs-text evidence conflict**, not conversational deference | Corrupted text is contextual evidence, not necessarily a socially framed user demand |
| [Liu et al., CVPR 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Liu_Unveiling_the_Ignorance_of_MLLMs_Seeing_Clearly_Answering_Incorrectly_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf) | **Strong fixed-image pairing:** correct positive question vs misleading negative question about same visual content | Paired response accuracy, visual attention and confidence; 15 MLLMs, 12 question categories | **Yes for prompted false premises**, but not sequential corrections | Rephrased questions alter pragmatics and may induce negative-question bias independently of social compliance |

## Outcome comparability: what can and cannot be pooled

- **Objective initial/final answer flips** require a baseline answer and a follow-up challenge that is independently adjudicated valid/invalid. Most papers above do *not* generate that exact paired trajectory; they use consensus, independent voting, judge accuracy, or image/text conflicts. Consequently their outcomes should be reported in separate narrative rows, not forced into a single pooled sycophancy rate.
- **NeurIPS debate papers:** Estornell addresses majority-with-misconception convergence, Choi evaluates voting versus debate, Kenton evaluates persuasion of a judge assigned correct/incorrect positions, and Hu evaluates judging stability. They answer four *different* questions and should not be presented as four independent replications of the same sycophancy experiment.
- **CVPR visual papers:** Deng tests *which modality wins* under contradictory textual context, whereas Liu tests *why correctly perceived image content still leads to wrong answers to misleading questions*. These are complementary but not equivalent to Pi et al.'s visual answer reversal under user suggestions or the acceptance of valid feedback.
- **Apology and acknowledgement:** none of the six directly operationalizes apology appropriateness, responsibility attribution, or the post-correction dialogue act. For that domain use [Alnasser et al. EMNLP 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.1595/), [SaFeRDialogues ACL 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.447/), [Grounding Gaps NAACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.348/), and [COLM 2026 retraction](https://proceedings.colm.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/133239b0506b84c802a12b0e5a764a17-Abstract-Conference.html); detailed full-text assessment of those sources is a separate activity, not a checkbox silently marked done.

## Bias/confound checklist applied to the six designs

| Domain | Specific evidence supporting judgment | Next-stage extraction needed |
|---|---|---|
| Selection and dataset construction | NeurIPS papers select specific reasoning/debate benchmarks; CVPR papers construct varied text or paired questions | Complete benchmark composition, leakage precautions and sample sizes by condition |
| Construct separation | Controlled false/valid advocates in Kenton; independent majority vote in Choi; cross-modal corruption in Deng | Independent evidence validity, exact manipulation and confidence for every arm |
| Outcome validity | Exact-match tasks, LLM-judge tasks, judge correctness, answer preference ratios and visual-answer accuracy differ | Raw denominators, uncertainty intervals, rater agreement and metric definitions |
| Comparators | All six include relevant alternative conditions, but costs/rounds/temperature and model versions differ | Matched compute, models, prompts and number of samples per intervention |
| Human external validity | Five studies are model-centric; Kenton uses weak LLM judge proxies for oversight | Human-judgment replication and real-world follow-up measurements |
| Selective reporting | Publisher pages and PDFs are available; this audit has not reconstructed every experiment/negative result | Supplementary materials, ablations, code/data availability and reported null effects |

**Conclusion:** The six papers support an *evidence-sensitive narrative comparison*, not an aggregate effect-size claim. More importantly, the controlled visual evidence studies and a correct/incorrect advocate design give the survey a stronger experimental foundation than venue prestige alone. This appraisal demonstrates methodological analysis of the inspected subset while explicitly stating what has not been extracted.
